1. Put the [chickpeas](i:chickpeas) and [baking soda](i:baking_soda) in a [saucepan](tool:saucepan), cover by several inches of water and bring to a boil over high heat. Keep boiling, lowering the heat if needed, for about [20 min](t:20m), until the chickpeas look bloated, their skins are falling off and they are very soft. Drain in a [strainer](tool:strainer) and run cold water over them for about 30 seconds. Set aside — no need to peel.

2. While the chickpeas cook, combine the [lemon juice](i:lemon), [garlic](i:garlic) and [salt](i:salt) in a [food processor](tool:food_processor). Process until the garlic is very finely chopped, then let the mixture rest for at least [10 min](t:10m) so the garlic mellows.

3. Add the [tahini](i:tahini) and blend until thick and creamy, scraping down any tahini stuck to the sides and bottom of the processor as needed.

4. With the processor running, drizzle in 2 tablespoons of [ice water](i:ice_water). Scrape down the sides and blend until the mixture is ultra smooth, pale and creamy. If your tahini was extra-thick, add 1 to 2 more tablespoons of ice water.

5. Add the [cumin](i:cumin) and the drained [chickpeas](i:chickpeas). While blending, drizzle in the [olive oil](i:olive_oil). Blend until super smooth, scraping down the sides as needed, about 2 minutes. Add more ice water by the tablespoon if you want it creamier.

6. Taste and adjust — add more [salt](i:salt) or [lemon juice](i:lemon) to taste.

7. Scrape the hummus into a serving bowl and use the back of a spoon to make nice swooshes on top. Drizzle with [olive oil](i:olive_oil), scatter [parsley](i:parsley) and [sumac](i:sumac), and serve. Leftovers keep, covered in the fridge, for up to 1 week.
